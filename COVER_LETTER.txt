Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript titled "Post-Intensive Care Syndrome (PICS) in adult survivors: A systematic review and meta-analysis of assessment tools, risk factors, and long-term outcomes" for consideration for publication in BMC Critical Care.

PICS represents a significant and often underrecognized burden affecting ICU survivors worldwide. Despite growing clinical awareness, substantial heterogeneity exists in assessment methodologies, outcome definitions, and reporting standards. This systematic review and meta-analysis addresses this critical gap by synthesizing evidence from 22 cohort studies (N = 8,487) and providing pooled estimates across 10 key domains using rigorous random-effects meta-analysis.

KEY CONTRIBUTIONS:

• First systematic review with integrated meta-analysis quantifying PICS outcomes: pooled 6-minute walk test distance of 406 m (95% CI 396–417), EQ-5D index 0.79 (0.70–0.87), and HADS scores indicating substantial psychological burden.

• Comprehensive assessment of 22 standardized instruments across cognitive, psychological, physical, and quality-of-life domains, highlighting measurement non-standardization as a critical barrier to PICS care.

• Quantification of high heterogeneity (I² = 82–98%) that directly reflects the absence of unified assessment frameworks—a core finding supporting the need for standardized protocols.

• Evidence-based synthesis of risk factors (mechanical ventilation, delirium, sedation) and preventive strategies (ABCDEF bundle, early mobilization) with clear implications for clinical practice.

• Rigorous adherence to PRISMA 2020 and STROBE guidelines, with full reproducibility through provided datasets and meta-analysis code.

WHY BMC CRITICAL CARE:

BMC Critical Care's commitment to systematic reviews, high methodological standards, and rapid open-access dissemination aligns perfectly with our manuscript's scope and audience. This work will enable critical care clinicians, researchers, and policymakers to implement standardized PICS assessment and management protocols.

NOVEL ASPECTS:

This is the first systematic review combining quantitative meta-analysis with comprehensive assessment of PICS instruments. Previous reviews were qualitative only. Our meta-analysis directly addresses the measurement standardization gap, providing actionable pooled estimates for clinical practice and future research.
